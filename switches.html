Omada Network Switches

Switch Tiers

From stackable L3 campus core switches to plug-and-play unmanaged models, every tier is engineered for a specific role in your network architecture.

Campus L3

Stackable Layer 3 switches with OSPF, VRRP, redundant power, and up to 1440W PoE for core and distribution layers.

Aggregation

Agile (Easy Managed)

Affordable cloud-managed switches ideal for surveillance, small retail, and simple business networks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about Omada network switches, PoE, and managed vs. unmanaged switching.

Managed switches, like most Omada models, offer greater control over your network. You can configure features like VLANs for security, QoS to prioritize traffic, and monitor network health. Unmanaged switches are simple plug-and-play devices for basic network expansion with no configuration options.

While not required (switches can be managed in standalone mode via a web interface or CLI), using an Omada SDN Controller (hardware, software, or cloud-based) unlocks centralized management, allowing you to control your entire network of switches, access points, and gateways from a single dashboard.

Yes. PoE ports are smart and will only supply power if they detect a PoE-compatible device. If you plug in a non-PoE device like a laptop or printer, the port will function as a standard data-only Ethernet port without any risk of damage.

These standards define the maximum power that can be delivered over an Ethernet cable. PoE (802.3af) delivers up to 15.4W. PoE+ (802.3at) delivers up to 30W, suitable for devices like VoIP phones and dual-band APs. PoE++ (802.3bt) is the latest standard, delivering 60W (Type 3) or 90-100W (Type 4), required for high-power devices like pan-tilt-zoom cameras and WiFi 6/7 access points.
